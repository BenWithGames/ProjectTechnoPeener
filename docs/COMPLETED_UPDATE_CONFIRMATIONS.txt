Completed-update confirmation processing

The prior two passing recordings showed zero drains at the late clock check.
This build adds a bounded owner-thread drain after a successful battle update.
ReservedBattleDriver invokes publication only after tick_reserved is consumed.
RawPredictionCoordinator first acknowledges exactly the revision and endpoint
used by that update, then publishes that completed revision, then drains.

Arrivals during capture/replay no longer wait for the next clock call to enter
the raw history and scheduler. Archived truth for the newly available scheduler
head is also submitted. No receiver-thread native writes, extra native calls,
sleep, additional prediction frames, or changes to sampling are introduced.

An arrival after replay is NOT acknowledged as applied. A mismatch leaves the
new revision dirty and the scheduler responsible for correction on its next
normal update or round settlement. There is no extra replay inside this drain.
Round settlement must still reconcile before retirement. Conflicts fail closed.
The existing partial-input-consumption guard and queue bound of 32 remain.

Reports add continuous_completed_receipt_schema=1, completed_receipt_checks,
completed_receipts_drained and completed_confirmations_submitted (all prefixed
continuous_). These are per-session counters, also present on retired rounds.
Keep using receive_to_drain, drain_to_scheduler and receive_to_prediction timing
distributions alongside correction depth and outer-update timings. Future-frame
truth still waits for its assigned consumption frame. This does not remove
network transit time and does not promise a fixed latency or FPS improvement.

LiveRawIntegrationTest covers both slots, arrivals after simulation, dirty state
not being falsely acknowledged, settlement replay, future truth admission,
conflicting late packets, and long-running delayed-input/retirement integration.
The existing native hook boundaries are reused; no new binary assumption or
Ghidra-derived offset is required. Fixed one-frame delay and three-frame limit,
exact restoration verification, resource ownership and round checks remain.
